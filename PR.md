This PR rewrites DeepSeek V4's mHC write-back backward as one Triton kernel, rewrites the mHC pre-pass backward as a gate kernel and a streams kernel with an opt-in TF32 dot mode, and routes the `hc_head` collapse through DeepGEMM's TF32 prenorm GEMM by zero-padding its 4-row projection to the kernel's row multiple of 8. At 31078 tokens per GPU the pre-pass backward drops from 2.878 to 1.488 ms per call, the write-back backward from 1.148 to 0.952 ms, and the `hc_head` forward from 3.135 to 0.610 ms.

The write-back kernel reads the output gradient, the residual streams and the sublayer output once and writes all four gradients (the old Megatron pair read the `(tokens, 4, hidden)` gradient twice), following DeepSeek's TileKernels design rewritten in Triton, nothing vendored. The pre-pass backward reduces the collapse gradient into gate gradients in a token-blocked pass and writes the input gradient plus the accumulated weight gradient from the same read of the streams in a hidden-slice pass. `PRIME_RL_DSV4_MHC_BWD_PRECISION=tf32` selects round-to-nearest TF32 dots in the backward, which halves the TF32 gradient error versus the MMA's truncation, and the kernels always honor the flag. `PRIME_RL_DSV4_MHC_PAD_PROJECTION=0` restores the multiple-of-8 gate and keeps `hc_head` on the IEEE Triton path. Traces from the campaign's tip profiling run (6 layers, 2 micro batches per step, 4 steps) show the rewrite per micro-batch window: the old pre-backward pair `_bwd_kernel` + `_bwd_weight_kernel` (26 calls, 37.3 ms summed in the main trace) is replaced by `_bwd_gate_kernel` + `_bwd_input_weight_kernel` (26 calls, 18.4 ms), the one-kernel `_post_bda_bwd_kernel` runs the write-back backward (12 calls, median 0.929 ms), and the padded `deep_gemm::sm90_tf32_hc_prenorm_gemm_impl<8u,...>` variant appears alongside the unchanged N=24 layer kernel.

The numbers come from the DSv4 stack A/B campaign on the 6-layer perf proxy (a DeepSeek V4 Flash checkpoint, 262144-token rows, cp 8, ep 8, full activation checkpointing, 20 steps of 8 micro batches, force-balanced routing), run on H200 with FP8 blockwise and on B300 with MXFP8, each paired against its own main arm on the same node. This campaign arm runs with the halo flag off and `PRIME_RL_DSV4_MHC_BWD_PRECISION=tf32` set on both hardware generations. The step-time metric is `time/forward_backward`, the table shows medians over steps 4 to 20, and a negative delta is faster. The campaigns use different MFU peak conventions, so the delta, not the MFU magnitude, is the comparable quantity across hardware. B300 repeat floors were 0.04 to 0.12 percent.

The campaign arm also carries the earlier optimization levels of this series, so the arm-minus-main delta is cumulative: -3.66% at 4 layers and -3.79% at 6 layers, and the chain marginal against the previous arm is about -1.5% and -1.2% (the tf32 backward is most of it). The campaign's same-code repeat floors are 0.15 to 0.25%. The 43-layer projection puts a step at 88.87 s versus main's 93.38 s, about -4.9%. Peak memory adds nothing beyond the previous arm's footprint (90.65 vs 90.74 GiB at 4 layers, 111.15 vs 111.24 at 6): the +3.4 GiB versus main is the footprint the q norm + RoPE level already introduced. Compile bar: identical Dynamo counts, +1 one-time DeepGEMM variant (the padded `hc_head`), and Triton cubins drop from 1020 to 818 (one kernel replaces two in the write-back, and the fused paths replace Triton variants). Exactly zero vLLM-op fallback warnings appeared in the campaign runs. On B300 the same cumulative delta is -4.29 percent at 6 layers, ahead of H200's -3.79, with the 43-layer projection at 49.6 s versus main's 51.3 s, and the B300 compile bar matches the H200 one (zero recompiles, zero graph breaks, the same +1 DeepGEMM variant, cubins at or below the main arm).

Numerics: with bf16 gradients (what FSDP produces) the tf32 backward sits at the bf16 output floor, 1.67e-3 relative Frobenius against fp64 versus 1.66e-3 for the old IEEE path, and in the fp32-output view the round-to-nearest dots cost 1.83e-4 against IEEE's 7.9e-6, 2x better than the old kernels at TF32 truncation (3.66e-4). The write-back gradients keep fp32 math with one rounding (fp32 outputs at 8.3e-8, bf16 outputs at the bf16 floor, not bitwise to the old pair because the fp32 summation order changes). `hc_head` keeps the same 1.659e-3 relative error against fp64 with 0.8% of bf16 outputs one ulp off the Triton path (different fp32 accumulation order), and an fp32 `fn` would hit TF32 truncation, which the bf16 parameters FSDP hands over never do. The full-model forward logprob A/B for the `hc_head` arm measures mean |dlogprob| 2.1e-4, p90 5.7e-6, KL 8e-6 both ways, far below the established floor (median |dlogprob| about 0.0014, p90 about 0.135), and the pre-backward tf32 arm sits at the bf16 floor under FSDP.

The new kernels' IEEE mode is not shippable as is: at `input_precision="ieee"` the four fp32 weight-gradient accumulators spill registers and the pre backward runs 53.6 ms per call at N=24, 19x slower than the old pair, so this level ships with `PRIME_RL_DSV4_MHC_BWD_PRECISION=tf32` as the opt-in under `matmul_precision = "highest"` and the IEEE register-spill fix is a follow-up. The TF32 backward already ran on B300 in the MXFP8 campaign at -4.29 percent at 6 layers, and whether it ships is decided by the B300 RL run. A fourth piece, fusing the sublayer input RMSNorm into the pre-pass forward, measured as a net regression end to end and is left out.

### Verification

- Unit tests on H200: the write-back output and all four gradients against fp64 for 2 and 4 streams (8 cases), the pre-pass selection (36 tests), the post-pass selection (8 tests), and the padded `hc_head` re-verified under the commit.
- Op bench at 31078 tokens per GPU: pre backward old ieee 2.878 ms, old tf32 1.623, new tf32 1.488 at N=24, write-back backward 1.148 to 0.952, `hc_head` forward Triton 3.135 to padded DeepGEMM 0.610 (vLLM's TileLang kernel 0.842 for reference).
- Full-model forward logprob A/Bs for the non-bitwise pieces, at or far below the established floor.
