This PR adds `model.dsv4_cp_kv_exchange = "halo"`, which makes each context-parallel rank of DeepSeek V4 attention receive only the few hundred rows before its shard from its left neighbor, pool only the compressed entries whose last token it holds, and all-gather the pooled entries, instead of all-gathering the whole row's sliding-window keys and compressor projections and pooling every entry of the row. Per layer and micro batch this cuts CP traffic about 19x for CSA and 270x for HCA and removes 7/8 of the pooling work.

The halo is `max(sliding_window - 1, 2 * compress_rate - 1)` rows (255 for V4 Flash), exchanged as a ring `all_to_all_single` whose autograd sends the halo gradients back. Rank pools go into a fixed-capacity buffer, so the all-gather has one size and no host sync, and the compressors return the same sequence-ordered entries as before, so the indexer, the HCA picks and the sparse-attention index contract are unchanged. The default stays `"all_gather"`, and pooling moves into `DeepseekV4Compressor._pool`, shared by both paths. Traces from the campaign's profiling runs (all 6 layers, 2 micro batches per step, 4 steps) show the mechanism: the main arm runs 46 `ncclDevKernel_AllGather_RING_LL` calls (55.7 ms summed) plus 10 Triton `all_gather_cat` concat kernels per micro-batch forward+backward window, while the halo arm runs 24 AllGather calls (27.0 ms), moves the key and value exchange onto 125 `ncclDevKernel_SendRecv` ring-shift calls (median 0.175 ms each), and drops the `all_gather_cat` kernels entirely. Summed NCCL time falls 396.0 to 329.3 ms per window and exposed NCCL (no other kernel running) falls 354.0 to 294.9 ms, 14.9% to 13.1% of the window.

The numbers come from the DSv4 stack A/B campaign on the 6-layer perf proxy (a DeepSeek V4 Flash checkpoint, 262144-token rows, cp 8, ep 8, full activation checkpointing, 20 steps of 8 micro batches, force-balanced routing), run twice: on H200 with FP8 blockwise, recomputed from the raw metrics in `~/tmp/dsv4-stack-attribution.md`, and on B300 with MXFP8, the production-representative path on that hardware, paired against its own main arm on the same node. The step-time metric is `time/forward_backward`, the table shows medians over steps 4 to 20, the post-compile window, and a negative delta is faster. The B300 MFU column divides FP8 work by the dense BF16 peak 2.25e15 FLOP/s while the H200 campaign used a different peak convention, so cross-hardware deltas, not MFU magnitudes, are the comparable quantity. B300 same-protocol repeat floors were 0.04 to 0.12 percent.

The campaign arm also carries the earlier optimization levels of this series, so the arm-minus-main delta is cumulative: -4.19% at 4 layers and -5.74% at 6 layers with the flag on, and the chain marginal against the previous arm (same runner, halo on vs off) is -3.46% and -5.19%. The campaign's same-code repeat floors are 0.15 to 0.25%, so both marginals are 15x or more the floor. The 43-layer projection puts a step at 83.80 s versus main's 93.38 s, about -10%. Peak memory falls 2.65 GiB at 4 layers and 1.74 GiB at 6 (the rank never materializes the full row). In the campaign's tip frame, adding halo plus the sink-gradient flag on top of the nearly complete arm is worth a further -1.227 s (-9.05%) at L6, a delta confounded with that flag and not additive with the standalone marginal. The win also holds with many short documents (about 256 documents per row, lengths 128 to 1920): 13.040 to 12.770 s per 4-layer step, where both arms pay the same many-docs cost. On B300 the same arm-minus-main delta is -6.82 percent with the 43-layer projection at 45.5 s versus main's 51.3 s, the win larger than H200's, consistent with a communication-heavy change favoring the faster interconnect, and B300 peak memory falls 2.34 GiB at 6 layers. B300 compile bar: zero Dynamo recompiles and zero graph breaks in every run, all compilation in step 1, Triton cubins at or below the main arm, DeepGEMM variants unchanged at 3. H200 compile bar: identical Dynamo counts and zero graph breaks in both arms, the halo arm compiles 65 more one-time Triton cubins in step 1, and neither campaign arm nor the trace run hit a step-1 hang.

Numerics: in eager mode the attention outputs and all compressed entries are bitwise equal to the all-gather path at cp 8 with documents crossing rank edges. Under `torch.compile` the sliding and CSA layers stay bitwise, while HCA entries flip by one bf16 ulp in a few entries because Inductor autotunes the 128-slot pooling reduction per graph and the halo graph pools 8x fewer entries (bitwise again with `TORCHINDUCTOR_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`). Compressor gradients differ at rounding level because the cross-rank sum now happens before the pooling backward instead of after it, within the run-to-run spread the attention backward's atomics already cause. A full-model forward logprob A/B (43 layers, cp 8, 262144 tokens, one 32768-token document per CP shard) puts the change at or below the known floor for a rounding-level change: mean |dlogprob| 0.036, p50 0.0007, p90 0.111, 0.12% of tokens above 1 nat, mean logprob -0.8535 vs -0.8538, against a bitwise-identical run-to-run rerun and a floor of mean |dlogprob| 0.046, p50 0.0014, p90 0.135, 0.20% above 1 nat.

Known flake, reframed by the stack campaign: the one step-1 hang this workstream saw in an early halo launch is a trainer-level deadlock, not a halo-specific failure. Across the campaign the hang occurred only on plain main: 3 hangs in 8 plain-main 6-layer launches (runner D attempt 1, runner E attempts 1 and 2), and 0 in every stack arm, tip arm, 4-layer main, or trace run. The signature is identical every time: every rank logs exactly 1 Dynamo recompile (checkpoint_wrapper forward, guard KeyError on `self_attn.compressor`) and then produces no further output, GPUs sit idle with about 42 GiB resident, and no metrics rows are ever written. It is not node-specific (hangs on two nodes, with a plain-main 6-layer run also succeeding on one of them), and every hang is on origin/main a566e5d8e, which includes #4314, while the previous night's 6-layer runs one commit behind never hung, so the clean test is the same run at f98f9937a, pre-#4314. Evidence: `~/tmp/dsv4-stack-watchdog-dumps/` (main-L6-E-hung1, main-L6-E-hung2) and the preserved run dirs main-L6-D-hung1, main-L6-E-hung1, main-L6-E-hung2. Because the deadlock is unexplained, the halo path stays opt-in and `"all_gather"` remains the default. A capture-armed campaign ran the workstream's original hanging config twelve more times with `PYTHONFAULTHANDLER=1`, `TORCH_NCCL_ENABLE_MONITORING=1` and `TORCH_NCCL_DUMP_ON_TIMEOUT=1` plus a spin-signature watchdog, and the hang never recurred (10 clean 20-step runs, 2 aborted without a verdict).
