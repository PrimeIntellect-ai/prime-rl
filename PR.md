This PR adds `model.dsv4_cp_kv_exchange = "halo"`, which makes each context-parallel rank of DeepSeek V4 attention receive only the few hundred rows before its shard from its left neighbor, pool only the compressed entries whose last token it holds, and all-gather the pooled entries, instead of all-gathering the whole row's sliding-window keys and compressor projections and pooling every entry of the row. Per layer and micro batch this cuts CP traffic about 19x for CSA and 270x for HCA and removes 7/8 of the pooling work.

The halo is `max(sliding_window - 1, 2 * compress_rate - 1)` rows (255 for V4 Flash), exchanged as a ring `all_to_all_single` whose autograd sends the halo gradients back. Rank pools go into a fixed-capacity buffer, so the all-gather has one size and no host sync, and the compressors return the same sequence-ordered entries as before, so the indexer, the HCA picks and the sparse-attention index contract are unchanged. The default stays `"all_gather"`, and pooling moves into `DeepseekV4Compressor._pool`, shared by both paths. Traces from the campaign's profiling runs (all 6 layers, 2 micro batches per step, 4 steps) show the mechanism: the main arm runs 46 `ncclDevKernel_AllGather_RING_LL` calls (55.7 ms summed) plus 10 Triton `all_gather_cat` concat kernels per micro-batch forward+backward window, while the halo arm runs 24 AllGather calls (27.0 ms), moves the key and value exchange onto 125 `ncclDevKernel_SendRecv` ring-shift calls (median 0.175 ms each), and drops the `all_gather_cat` kernels entirely. Summed NCCL time falls 396.0 to 329.3 ms per window and exposed NCCL (no other kernel running) falls 354.0 to 294.9 ms, 14.9% to 13.1% of the window.

The numbers come from the DSv4 stack A/B campaign on the 6-layer perf proxy (a DeepSeek V4 Flash checkpoint, 262144-token rows, cp 8, ep 8, full activation checkpointing, 20 steps of 8 micro batches, force-balanced routing), run on H200 with FP8 blockwise and on B300 with MXFP8, each paired against its own main arm on the same node. The step-time metric is `time/forward_backward`, the table shows medians over steps 4 to 20, and a negative delta is faster. B300 repeat floors were 0.04 to 0.12 percent.

| run | median time/forward_backward s, H200 | median time/forward_backward s, B300 | delta vs main, H200 | delta vs main, B300 | tok/s per GPU (B300) | peak GiB (B300) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| main | 14.557 | 8.227 | - | - | 29937 | 109.17 |
| l3 (halo on) | 13.721 | 7.666 | -5.74% | -6.82% | 32116 | 106.83 |

The arm-minus-main delta is cumulative through the stack: -5.74% on H200 and -6.82% on B300 at 6 layers, the win larger on B300 consistent with a communication-heavy change favoring the faster interconnect, with the 43-layer projection at 83.8 versus 93.4 s on H200 and 45.5 versus 51.3 s on B300. Peak memory falls 1.7 GiB on H200 and 2.3 GiB on B300 at 6 layers, since a rank never materializes the full row. Numerics: eager mode is bitwise equal to the all-gather path, under `torch.compile` the HCA entries move by at most one bf16 ulp, and the full-model logprob A/B sits at or below the known floor for a rounding-level change. The one observed step-1 hang is a trainer-level deadlock on plain main (3 of 8 plain-main 6-layer launches, never in any stack or tip arm), so the halo path stays opt-in with `"all_gather"` as the default.
